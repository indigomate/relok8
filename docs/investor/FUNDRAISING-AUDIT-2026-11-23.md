# Relok8 fundraising audit

**Reviewed:** 23 November 2026  
**Deck:** `Relok8-Pre-Seed-Pitch-Deck.pdf`  
**Purpose:** decide whether the current deck is ready for a broad capital process.

## Verdict

**Do not send the current PDF broadly yet.**

The deck is visually strong and its narrative is coherent, but it still reads as a product-readiness deck rather than an investment-evidence deck. It is suitable for friendly investor discovery calls after the two visible editing instructions are removed. It is not yet suitable for a high-volume VC process.

| Dimension | Score | Assessment |
|---|---:|---|
| Narrative and visual craft | 82/100 | Clear problem, wedge, workflow, pricing and raise; professional design. |
| External send readiness | 62/100 | Blocked by editing instructions, missing traction and thin founder evidence. |
| Company fundraising readiness | 58/100 | Live product exists, but marketplace liquidity, willingness to pay and repeatability remain unproven. |

The difference between these scores matters: a good-looking deck can earn a meeting, but it cannot substitute for commercial evidence.

## Immediate blockers

### 1. Visible editing instructions

- Slide 13 contains a red instruction to add verified users, listings, enquiries and handovers.
- Slide 15 contains a footer asking for confirmation of the founder email and founder-market experience.

These are internal production notes and should never appear in an investor-facing PDF.

### 2. Traction is not shown

The deck proves that software was built. It does not yet prove that a marketplace exists. Before a broad process, add a dated, sourced traction block containing only verified figures:

- registered and verified users;
- live listings by city;
- qualified enquiries per active listing;
- landlord invitations and approvals;
- completed handovers;
- median days from listing to handover;
- first paying landlord, pilot or signed design partner;
- user interviews and willingness-to-pay tests.

If the figures are still zero or too early, say so honestly and replace the traction slide with a 90-day validation plan. Do not call the product commercially “working” until the move workflow is used repeatedly.

### 3. Founder-market fit is too generic

“Product builder” is execution evidence, not founder-market fit. The deck needs a concise answer to:

1. Why did Indiana encounter or understand this problem?
2. What specific insight does Indiana have that listing portals miss?
3. Why can Indiana recruit the first users, landlords and institutional partners?
4. What personal or professional evidence shows unusual persistence or capability?

Only add facts that can be defended in diligence.

### 4. The market slide is a beachhead slide, not a TAM slide

International-student counts help explain the first cohort, but investors still need a money-sized market model. Add a bottom-up model using:

`annual addressable renter moves × realistic paid conversion × net revenue per completed tenancy`

Then show a city-level serviceable market for the first two Polish cities. Separate:

- beachhead users;
- total renter moves;
- addressable landlord-approved takeovers;
- realistic three-year serviceable revenue.

### 5. Revenue scenarios need an evidence bridge

The 49 PLN + 49 PLN model is memorable, but the Poland/Europe/global scenarios are volume illustrations rather than forecasts. Add the bridge from the first city to 20,000 annual completed tenancies:

- acquisition channel;
- listing-to-enquiry rate;
- enquiry-to-landlord-invitation rate;
- approval rate;
- paid-tenancy duration;
- landlord churn and gross margin.

Until those variables are measured, label the figures as scenarios everywhere.

### 6. The raise budget needs a founder-runway explanation

The current 15% “founder runway + admin” allocation equals €37,500 over 18 months. Because it combines founder compensation with administration, an investor cannot tell whether Indiana can actually become full-time.

Split founder salary, legal/accounting and contingency. The operating model should show monthly cash burn, taxes, contractor or employee costs and at least a three-month buffer. The deck may keep the high-level allocation once it reconciles to that model.

### 7. Competition should name the alternatives

The category view is sensible, but investors will compare Relok8 with actual behaviour and products. Name the most relevant alternatives in diligence material:

- Facebook and WhatsApp housing groups;
- OLX and Otodom;
- HousingAnywhere, Spotahome and similar cross-border rental products;
- landlord/property-management software;
- manual bank transfer, deposit and identity workflows.

The claim should be that Relok8 coordinates the move and approval workflow—not that no competitors exist.

## What is already strong

- A real production product exists.
- The three-sided lease-takeover wedge gives each party a reason to act.
- The 49 PLN once + 49 PLN monthly model is simple.
- Roadmap claims distinguish current coordination from future regulated financial services.
- Poland is a credible capital-efficient entry market.
- The broader renter positioning avoids limiting the company to students.
- The €250k ask is within the range of angels and several pre-seed investors.

## Recommended investor versions

### 10–12 slide send deck

1. Cover and one-line category.
2. Problem and founder insight.
3. Lease-takeover wedge and why now.
4. Product proof.
5. Traction or 90-day validation evidence.
6. Market: bottom-up Poland, Europe and global expansion.
7. Business model and early willingness-to-pay evidence.
8. Go-to-market and city liquidity mechanics.
9. Competition and defensibility.
10. Team and founder-market fit.
11. Raise, milestones and use of funds.
12. Close/contact.

Move detailed workflow, roadmap, revenue scenarios, sources and regulatory sequencing to an appendix.

### Six-minute PARP deck

Prepare separate Polish and English `.pptx` versions. Emphasise Smart City relevance, international expansion, product readiness and measurable mission outcomes. The general investor deck should not be submitted unchanged.

## Priority evidence sprint before broad outreach

| Priority | Evidence | Minimum useful result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Founder story | Three defensible sentences explaining why Indiana owns this problem. |
| 2 | Customer discovery | 20 dated interviews across renters, outgoing tenants and landlords. |
| 3 | Supply | 25 legitimate active listings in one city. |
| 4 | Workflow | 10 landlord invitations and 3 documented approvals. |
| 5 | Completion | First 3 completed handovers with consented evidence. |
| 6 | Willingness to pay | At least 5 landlords explicitly accept or reject the 49 PLN/month offer. |
| 7 | Institutional demand | Two written design-partner or pilot commitments. |

## Fundraising recommendation

Run a **two-track process**:

1. **Capital track:** angels and pre-seed funds for the €250k round and founder runway.
2. **Non-dilutive/network track:** PARP programmes for foreign-market access, pilots and investor introductions.

Do not describe travel, acceleration or reimbursement programmes as money available for founder salary. Do not start a broad VC blast until the send deck is clean and the first commercial evidence is available.
